# Image-quality parity between the two species

Answers reviewer comment 1: *"is there a measure like SNR that can empirically
show there is no significant difference between the species?"*

**Short answer: no, and the data says the opposite.** On the face-ROI comparison,
11 of 12 quality metrics differ significantly between the two species' datasets
after multiplicity correction, **zero** metrics are statistically equivalent
within a half-SD margin, and effective resolution separates the two species
*completely* — every mauritius identity is sharper-resolved than every rousettus
identity. The datasets are not comparable, so the species performance
comparison has to be controlled rather than assumed clean.

**And the gap has consequences.** Measured in a frozen ImageNet embedding, the
mauritius individuals look 2.7× more separable than the rousettus ones — but
once effective resolution is matched, that advantage largely disappears (see
[Intrinsic separability](#does-the-quality-gap-actually-matter-intrinsic-separability)).
The species difference we have been reporting is, to a substantial degree, a
difference in how big the bat's face was in the frame.

Generated by `scripts/measure_native_boxes.py` → `scripts/build_quality_battery.py`
→ `scripts/analyze_quality_parity.py`. Raw output: `outputs/quality/parity_report.json`.

## Why one number was not enough

The manifests already carry a quality proxy — variance of the Laplacian
(`bat_data.manifest.compute_quality`). It reports a 3.8× gap between species, and
it was tempting to read that as "mauritius images are much sharper". Laplacian
variance conflates three separate things:

- genuine sharpness / focus,
- **effective resolution** — an upscaled crop is smooth, and so scores low,
- **noise** — a noisy image scores *high*, because noise is high-frequency energy.

The battery separates them. The result is that both resolution *and* noise are
higher for mauritius, so the headline sharpness gap overstates the difference in
real detail.

## Method

- **Unit of analysis: the identity, never the image.** 520 vs 1092 images live
  inside 16 vs 12 individuals, and all images of one bat come from one video.
  A per-image test is pseudoreplication and returns p < 1e-30 for almost
  anything. Each identity contributes its median; n = 16 vs n = 12.
- **Difference test:** Mann-Whitney U, with Cliff's δ and Hedges' g as effect
  sizes, Benjamini-Hochberg corrected across the 12 metrics within each
  background.
- **Equivalence test:** TOST at a margin of |g| < 0.5, declared in advance —
  "differences below half a between-identity standard deviation do not matter".
  This is the only test that can *support* parity; a non-significant difference
  test cannot.
- **Three verdicts**, so an under-powered result is never silently read as
  parity: `different`, `equivalent`, or `inconclusive`.
- **Read the `green` rows.** Only the green-background variant has a recoverable
  face mask, so only there is every metric restricted to the face. On `original`
  and `random`, gradient measures partly report background scenery.

## Correction (2026-08-24): these are not the crops the models trained on

**The numbers below measure `aligned_224`; the 360-run k-fold sweep trained on
`not_augmented/`.** For mauritius those are byte-identical, so its figures carry
over unchanged. For **rousettus they are different images** — the two builds
produce visibly different crops (mean absolute pixel difference ≈ 39/255) — and
the rousettus training crops are *sharper*. Recomputing the identical battery on
the crops the models actually saw:

| metric (per-identity median ratio) | measured below (`aligned_224`) | on the trained crops (`not_augmented`) |
|---|---|---|
| `laplacian_var` | 3.78× | **3.21×** |
| `gradient_energy` | 2.08× | **1.87×** |
| `tenengrad` | 1.19× | **1.07×** |
| `noise_sigma_immerkaer` | 3.13× | **2.50×** |

So the species quality gap is real and in the same direction, but **smaller than
stated below** for every sharpness and noise measure. `native_side_px` is
unaffected: it is derived from the source frame, not the crop, so the resolution
result (1.97×, Cliff's δ = +1.00, complete separation) stands exactly as written.

This was found while calibrating the Phase 2A degradation arms, which had to be
matched against the trained crops — calibrating against the figures below would
have over-degraded mauritius. Anything that quotes this document as the quality
gap *of the trained models' data* should use the right-hand column.

### Reading it

1. **Effective resolution is the dominant, cleanest difference.** Median native
   head-box 614 px vs 312 px, Cliff's δ = **+1.00** — *complete* separation, no
   overlap whatsoever at identity level (mauritius minimum 441 px > rousettus
   maximum 407 px). 48.4% of rousettus crops are upscaled at 320 px against 0.2%
   of mauritius. This measurement independently reproduces the previously
   observed "~49% of rousettus upscaled at 320", which validates it.
2. **The sharpness metrics disagree in a way that confirms resolution, not focus,
   is the driver.** Ordered by how scale-sensitive each measure is:
   `gradient_energy` (1-pixel difference) 2.08×, `laplacian_var` (3×3) 3.78×,
   `tenengrad` (3×3 Sobel, least noise-sensitive) only 1.19× and *inconclusive*.
   A real focus difference would move all three together.
3. **mauritius is noisier, not cleaner** — 2.2× to 3.1× higher noise σ by two
   independent estimators. Since Laplacian variance counts noise as signal, part
   of that 3.78× "sharpness" advantage is actually sensor noise. mauritius was
   shot outdoors in daylight, rousettus indoors in dim light with (evidently)
   more aggressive denoising.
4. **rousettus is not simply the worse dataset.** It has *higher* contrast (both
   measures) and marginally higher entropy. The two datasets differ along several
   axes in opposing directions.
5. **Nothing is equivalent.** Every TOST p-value on green is ≥ 0.53; none comes
   near 0.05. Even where the difference test is inconclusive (`tenengrad`),
   equivalence is *not* established — that metric is simply under-powered at
   n = 16 vs 12.

## Does the quality gap actually matter? Intrinsic separability

The battery measures *acquisition* quality. The question that matters for
recognition is whether the individuals are distinguishable at all:

    signal = variation between individuals
    noise  = variation between images of the same individual

`scripts/analyze_separability.py` measures this in a **frozen ImageNet ResNet50**
— never a model trained on these bats — so the answer is a property of the
datasets, not of our architectures. Metrics on L2-normalised embeddings under
cosine geometry; intervals are delete-2 jackknives over identities (a bootstrap
is wrong here: drawing an identity twice creates two coincident clusters and
collapses every clustering metric). Green background.

| regime | design | d′ (mau / rou) | Fisher ratio | silhouette |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `raw` | 16 vs 12 ids, 520 vs 1092 imgs | 1.268 / 0.994 → **1.28×** | 1.120 / 0.418 → **2.68×** | 0.246 / 0.119 → **2.07×** |
| `matched_design` | 12 ids × 26 imgs each | 1.330 / 1.019 → **1.31×** | 1.205 / 0.444 → **2.71×** | 0.276 / 0.123 → **2.24×** |
| `matched_resolution` | 11 ids × 8 imgs, in-band only | 1.298 / 1.398 → **0.93×** | 1.366 / 0.857 → **1.59×** | 0.266 / 0.191 → **1.39×** |

Figure: `outputs/quality/figures/separability__both_video_green_resnet50_none.png`.
Raw output: `outputs/quality/separability.json`.

**Sample size explains nothing; resolution explains most of it.** Equalising
identity counts and images per identity (`matched_design`) leaves every ratio
essentially unchanged — so the apparent gap is not an artifact of rousettus
having more images inside fewer bats. Equalising *effective resolution* changes
all three metrics at once: the d′ gap disappears and mildly **reverses**
(rousettus 1.398 [1.237, 1.623] vs mauritius 1.298 [1.038, 1.464]), the Fisher
ratio gap falls from 2.7× to 1.6×, and the silhouette gap from 2.2× to 1.4×.

So "rousettus individuals are less distinctive" is substantially a **measurement
artifact of smaller faces in frame**, not an established biological fact.

Two honest caveats. The matched-resolution regime has only 11 identities × 8
images per species, so its intervals are wide and it is suggestive rather than
conclusive. And the three metrics disagree there: d′ (which standardises by the
spread of the similarity distributions) favours rousettus, while Fisher ratio and
silhouette (which describe cluster geometry) still favour mauritius. The
consistent claim is that matching resolution *shrinks* the gap substantially on
every metric; the claim that it fully eliminates it rests on d′ alone.

## Consequence for the species comparison

A raw mauritius-vs-rousettus performance difference cannot be attributed to the
animals. It is confounded by a ~2× effective-resolution gap, a noise gap, a
contrast gap, and (separately, from the manifests) by identity counts and
images-per-identity. **The matched arm is mandatory, not a robustness check.**

**A resolution-matched arm is constructible.** Although identity medians do not
overlap, individual images do: the common native-resolution band is
**[297.9, 623.3] px**, which contains 311/520 (59.8%) of mauritius images and
656/1092 (60.1%) of rousettus images. Restricting to it leaves

- **10 of 16** mauritius identities with ≥ 10 in-band images, and
- **11 of 12** rousettus identities with ≥ 10 in-band images,

which is enough for a matched-resolution training set on both sides. Combine
with equalised train/test identity counts and an images-per-identity cap to
isolate the species effect.

## Limitations to state in the write-up

- BRISQUE / NIQE / PIQE are **not** included. `piq` is not installed, and only
  BRISQUE is available there in any case; NIQE and PIQE would each need their own
  pristine-model parameters. They were dropped rather than approximated. Their
  absence costs little: they are trained on human opinion scores of natural
  photographs with synthetic distortions, so tight animal-face crops on a flat
  green background are outside their training distribution.
- `native_side_px` is the aligner's source-crop side derived from the
  segmentation mask's axis-aligned bounding box. The aligner itself uses the
  *rotated* box, so this is a close proxy, not an exact reproduction (it is the
  same quantity the dataset build's own upscale check uses).
- `snr` here is mean ÷ σ of ROI luminance, the textbook image SNR. It rewards
  flat regions, so for a textured subject it must be read together with the
  noise-σ estimators rather than on its own.
- n = 16 vs 12 identities gives limited power. `inconclusive` verdicts mean
  exactly that, and more identities would be needed to convert them either way.
