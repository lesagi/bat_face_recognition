\begin{abstract}
We present an open-set individual face recognition system for fruit bats, designed to
support longitudinal behavioural studies where every animal must be identified without
prior appearance in the training set.
Our primary methodological contribution is a rigorous \emph{identity-disjoint} evaluation
protocol: train, validation, and test splits are partitioned at the level of individual
identities so that no bat ever appears in more than one split.
We show that the widely-used alternative---random pair selection---inflates performance
dramatically: a Siamese network that achieves $F_1 = 0.99$ under the random-pair protocol
drops to $F_1 = 0.71$ under identity-disjoint evaluation on the same images.

We compare three deep-learning model families---a pair-based Siamese convolutional
network and two embedding-based angular-margin architectures (ArcFace and AdaFace built
on a ResNet-50 backbone)---across a factorial design covering two fruit-bat species
(\rousettus and \mauritius), three background conditions (green screen, original, and
random compositing), and small identity counts ($\le 13$ training individuals).
The Siamese network is the only family that verifies significantly across \emph{all}
conditions (pairwise-head \rocauc $0.67$--$0.86$; cosine \rocauc $0.82$--$0.96$).  The
embedding models, by contrast, are governed by the background: on random-composited
backgrounds they regress toward chance (rousettus ArcFace $0.665$, AdaFace $0.434$;
not statistically significant), but on coherent green-screen backgrounds they recover
sharply and can \emph{surpass} the Siamese network---AdaFace reaches \rocauc $0.902$ with
Top-1 $0.916$ on \mauritius green, at the same small identity count.
We therefore characterise the embedding-model shortfall as a \emph{background-sensitivity}
effect rather than a fixed small-identity ceiling: with stable backgrounds, angular-margin
embeddings transfer to unseen identities even at this scale, whereas random-background
nuisance variation---not identity count alone---is what collapses them.

All experiments are fully reproducible via a single-command pipeline and are tracked in
MLflow.
\end{abstract}
