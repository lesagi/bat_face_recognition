\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Two robust families, and one decisive covariate: background}

Two findings organise the corrected results.  First, the Siamese network is
\emph{robust}: it produces statistically significant verification (permutation test,
$p<0.05$) on every species and background, with pairwise-head ROC-AUC in
$0.67$--$0.86$ and cosine ROC-AUC in $0.82$--$0.96$.  Second, the embedding models
(ArcFace and AdaFace), once the AdaFace additive margin, ImageNet normalisation, and
matched $112$-px evaluation are corrected, are \emph{not} uniformly weak---their
behaviour is governed almost entirely by the background condition rather than by the
number of training identities.

This is the central revision relative to our earlier reading of these experiments.  We
had attributed the embedding models' weakness to a ``small-identity generalisation
wall.''  The corrected runs show that with $\le 13$ training identities the very same
models reach---and on Mauritius green \emph{exceed}---the Siamese network when the
background is coherent (AdaFace Mauritius green: ROC-AUC $0.902$, Top-1 $0.916$, mAP
$0.955$).  The factor that separates success from collapse is therefore not identity
count alone but whether the test imagery shares the controlled background of training.

\paragraph{Objective alignment still favours pairs under distribution shift.}
The structural reason the Siamese network is robust remains valid: it optimises a
\emph{pairwise verification} objective that matches the deployment criterion and never
binds identities to fixed classifier slots.  Angular-margin losses instead solve a
\emph{closed-set classification} problem and rely on the learned hypersphere geometry
transferring to unseen identities.  What the new data clarifies is that this transfer
succeeds at small identity counts \emph{when the input distribution is stable}, and
fails when random-background compositing injects nuisance variation that the
closed-set geometry has not been forced to factor out.

\subsection{Background sensitivity, not identity count, drives the gap}
\label{sec:identity_wall}

Three signals, re-examined across background conditions, converge on background as the
governing variable.

\paragraph{The train/test gap is background-dependent, not count-dependent.}
Training accuracy is near-perfect for the embedding models in \emph{every} condition, so
identity count alone cannot explain the test gap.  What moves the gap is the background:

With the identity count held fixed, moving from random to green backgrounds roughly
halves the ArcFace gap ($-0.304 \to -0.151$) and substantially reduces the AdaFace gap.
A pure identity-count bottleneck would not be relieved by changing the background.

\paragraph{Top-1 identification is at chance only on random backgrounds.}
With 3 unseen test identities, chance Top-1 is $0.33$.  On random backgrounds the
embedding models hover near chance (ArcFace rousettus $0.53$, AdaFace $0.27$), but on
green screen they are far above it (ArcFace rousettus $0.85$, Mauritius $0.74$; AdaFace
Mauritius $0.92$).  The embedding space \emph{does} acquire transferable structure for
novel identities---provided the background does not dominate the signal.

\paragraph{Optimization instability tracks the background too.}
The validation ROC-AUC curves for ArcFace and AdaFace on random backgrounds
(Figure~\ref{fig:learning_curves}) show high epoch-to-epoch variance and, for AdaFace,
decay toward chance, whereas the Siamese network converges smoothly.  On green/original
backgrounds the same losses converge to high, stable validation scores (Section
\ref{sec:experiments}).  The instability is thus a property of the random-background
optimisation landscape rather than an intrinsic small-class pathology.

\paragraph{Cross-species consistency points to background, not species.}
The pattern replicates across both species, which rules out species-specific causes:

Both species are weak on random compositing and both recover strongly on green screen.
Had identity count been the binding constraint, the green-screen runs---at the same
small counts---would not recover.

\paragraph{A softened role for identity count.}
None of this means identity count is irrelevant.  The literature is unambiguous that
angular-margin losses improve with many training identities~\cite{deng2019arcface,
kim2022adaface}, and our random-background runs remain the hardest precisely where
nuisance variation compounds limited identity diversity.  Our revised reading is more
specific: at this scale the \emph{binding} constraint we can demonstrate is background
coherence, and the apparent identity-count ``wall'' in earlier runs was confounded both
by the random-background condition and by the AdaFace-margin and evaluation-resolution
defects that this study corrects.  Disentangling residual identity-count effects from
background effects would require a controlled sweep of identity count at fixed
background, which we leave to future work.

\subsection{Background effects}

Across families, coherent backgrounds help, but the effect is far larger for the
embedding models than for the Siamese network.  On Mauritius the Siamese pairwise-head
ROC-AUC varies only modestly with background ($0.71$--$0.83$), whereas ArcFace swings
from $0.578$ (random) to $0.843$ (green) and AdaFace from $0.539$ to $0.902$
(Table~\ref{tab:mauritius}).  Two mechanisms are plausible: (1) random texture
compositing introduces visual clutter that competes with facial features in the input
field; (2) consistent backgrounds reduce domain shift within the test split.  The
markedly stronger sensitivity of the angular-margin geometry indicates that, unlike the
pairwise objective, it does not automatically learn to factor out background nuisance at
this data scale---an actionable finding, since background standardisation at capture
time is cheap relative to collecting many more identities.

\subsection{The leaky-vs-honest gap}

The 28-point F\textsubscript{1} gap between the random-pair protocol and the
identity-disjoint protocol (Table~\ref{tab:leakage}) is larger than typical published
figures for animal re-identification.  Practitioners are encouraged to audit their
evaluation protocols: if the same individual appears in both train and test, reported
numbers are not measuring deployed-scenario performance.

\subsection{Limitations}

\paragraph{Single-seed evaluation.}
All reported numbers come from single training runs.  The standard deviation of test
metrics across seeds is unknown and likely non-trivial for the embedding models given
the instability documented in Section~\ref{sec:identity_wall}: a repeated AdaFace run on
rousettus random produced test ROC-AUC $0.671$ versus the reported $0.434$ from an
identical configuration, underscoring that the random-background embedding numbers in
particular should be read as single-sample point estimates with wide implied
uncertainty.

\paragraph{No external validation set.}
The test identities were drawn from the same colony as the training identities.
Generalisation to bats from different colonies or imaging setups is untested.

\paragraph{No ablation on backbone pretraining.}
All embedding models used ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 weights.  Whether
domain-adaptive pretraining on a larger bat-face corpus would lower the identity-count
threshold is an open question.
