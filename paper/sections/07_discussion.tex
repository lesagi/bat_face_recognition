\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why pair-based models win at small identity counts}

The consistent advantage of the Siamese network over ArcFace and AdaFace across all
experimental conditions is not an architectural accident but a structural consequence of
the training objective.

Angular-margin losses optimise a \emph{closed-set classification} problem: at training
time the model learns to separate the specific identities it has seen, placing them into
distinct cones on the unit hypersphere.  The hope at test time is that the manifold
structure of bat-face space learned from a few training identities will generalise---that
unseen test identities will land in distinguishable regions of the same embedding space.
With 6 training identities and 512 embedding dimensions, the hypersphere has vastly more
capacity than necessary for the training task, and the resulting geometry is not
constrained to be meaningful for any particular arrangement of three unseen identities.

The Siamese network, by contrast, optimises a \emph{pairwise verification} objective
directly: the model is asked, for every training batch, whether two images show the same
bat or different bats.  The model does not need to assign identities to fixed slots; it
only needs to learn features that distinguish individuals in general.  This objective
is better aligned with the deployment task and generalises naturally to unseen identities,
as long as the discriminative features themselves transfer.

We expect this ordering to reverse as identity count increases: with hundreds of training
identities the angular manifold becomes well-constrained and embedding-based models
typically dominate~\cite{deng2019arcface, kim2022adaface}.  Our results define the
crossover regime as somewhere above $\sim\!12$ training identities for bat faces, a
threshold likely to shift with dataset size and pretrained backbone quality.

\subsection{Background effects}

The green-screen and original-background conditions outperform random-background
compositing for both species on the Siamese network ($0.925/0.922$ vs.\ $0.803$ on
mauritius; Table~\ref{tab:mauritius}).  Two explanations are plausible: (1) the random
texture compositing introduces more visual clutter that competes with facial features in
the 105$\times$105 input field; (2) training and testing under consistent backgrounds
(all green-screen or all original) reduces domain shift within the test split.

The \emph{embedding} models show a stronger relative improvement under green-screen
($0.761$ for ArcFace green vs.\ $0.526$ for ArcFace random on mauritius), suggesting
that the angular-margin geometry may be more sensitive to background variation than the
pairwise objective.

\subsection{The leaky-vs-honest gap}

The 24-point F\textsubscript{1} gap between the random-pair protocol and the
identity-disjoint protocol (Table~\ref{tab:leakage}) is larger than typical published
figures for animal re-identification.  Practitioners are encouraged to audit their
evaluation protocols: if the same individual appears in both train and test, reported
numbers are not measuring the deployed-scenario performance.

\subsection{Limitations}

\paragraph{Identity count.}
With at most 6 training identities, all results should be treated as characteristic of
the small-sample regime.  Top-1 identification accuracy near chance ($0.29$--$0.36$ out
of 3 test identities) for embedding models reflects this: the model cannot reliably assign
a test image to one of three unseen individuals via rank-1 retrieval, even when the
verification AUC is above $0.5$.  This is not a contradiction: AUC is a pairwise metric
and can be above chance even when identification fails.

\paragraph{Single-seed evaluation.}
All reported numbers come from single training runs.  The standard deviation of test
metrics across seeds is unknown for the pair-based model and likely non-trivial for the
embedding models given the instability visible in Figure~\ref{fig:arcface_collapse}~(right).

\paragraph{No external validation set.}
The test identities were drawn from the same colony as the training identities.
Generalisation to bats from different colonies or different imaging setups is untested.

\paragraph{No ablation on backbone pretraining.}
All embedding models used ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 weights.  The effect of
domain-adaptive pretraining (e.g.\ on a larger bat-face corpus) is left for future work.
