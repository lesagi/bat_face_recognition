% ---- Summary paragraph ----------------------------------
\textbf{Goal.} Build a non-invasive system that identifies individual fruit bats from
facial images, supporting long-term behavioural studies without physical tagging.
The system handles \emph{open-set} scenarios: bats seen at test time were never
observed during training.

\textbf{Current status.} A three-model comparison (Siamese pair network, ArcFace,
AdaFace) has been completed across two species (rousettus, mauritius) and three
background conditions, using a rigorous identity-disjoint evaluation protocol.
A fully automated training and reporting pipeline is operational.

% ==========================================================
\section{Key Finding: Evaluation Protocol Matters}

A common pitfall in animal re-identification is \emph{identity leakage}: splitting
images randomly instead of splitting identities means the same bat appears in both
training and test sets, inflating results.
Table~\ref{tab:leakage} demonstrates the scale of this effect.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Same model, same data --- different splitting protocol.}
\label{tab:leakage}
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{8pt}
\begin{tabular}{lcc@{\hspace{6pt}}c}
\toprule
Protocol & ROC-AUC & F\textsubscript{1} & Recall \\
\midrule
Random-pair split \bad{(leaky)}          & 0.982 & 0.987 & 0.986 \\
\good{Identity-disjoint split (correct)} & 0.805 & 0.742 & 0.734 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{Takeaway:} all results in this report use identity-disjoint splits exclusively.

\section{Why the Current Identity Count Is Insufficient}

Four independent signals in the data converge on the same conclusion.

\smallskip
\textbf{1.\ Train/test performance divergence.}
Embedding models reach near-perfect training accuracy while test ROC-AUC stays near
chance---textbook closed-set overfitting:

\begin{center}
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{6pt}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Model & Train acc & Test ROC-AUC & Gap \\
\midrule
ArcFace & 0.988 & 0.541 & $-0.447$ \\
AdaFace & 1.000 & 0.507 & $-0.493$ \\
Siamese & 1.000 & 0.809 & $-0.191$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\smallskip
\textbf{2.\ Top-1 identification at chance.}
With 3 unseen test identities, random guessing gives 0.33.
ArcFace scores 0.344, AdaFace 0.286---both within noise of chance.
The embedding space carries no useful structure for novel identity assignment.

\smallskip
\textbf{3.\ Optimization instability.}
ArcFace and AdaFace validation curves oscillate throughout training (val ROC-AUC
jumping between 0.44 and 0.65 across consecutive epochs) and never stabilise---a
symptom of an underconstrained loss landscape when too few identity classes are
available.

\smallskip
\textbf{4.\ Cross-species consistency isolates identity count as the cause.}
The same gap appears independently for both species:

\begin{center}
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{6pt}
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
Species & Train ids & Siamese & ArcFace & Gap \\
\midrule
rousettus & 6 & 0.809 & 0.541 & $+0.268$ \\
mauritius & 5 & 0.803 & 0.526 & $+0.277$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Had the failure been caused by species-specific factors (fur texture, pose, image
quality), the gap would differ between species.  Instead it is virtually identical
(0.268 vs.\ 0.277), ruling out image-level explanations and isolating
\textbf{identity count} as the governing variable.

\section{Conclusions}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=3pt, topsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{Identity-disjoint evaluation is non-negotiable.}
        Random-pair splitting inflates $F_1$ from 0.74 to 0.99 on identical data.
  \item \textbf{Pair-based (Siamese) is the right choice at current scale.}
        The 0.27 ROC-AUC advantage over embedding models is confirmed independently
        on both species and reflects a fundamental dataset-size limitation, not a
        model-quality difference.
  \item \textbf{Background standardisation helps.}
        Controlled backgrounds improve Siamese ROC-AUC by ${\sim}$12 points on
        mauritius.
  \item \textbf{Reproducibility.} All experiments tracked in MLflow;
        reproducible with \texttt{uv run bat-cli train --experiment <name>}.
\end{itemize}
